Yong Hye-in, the nominee for Minister of Gender Equality and Family, resigned on the 13th. The Democratic Party of Korea then launched a full-scale defense of Kim Seung-won, the nominee for Minister of Justice. Recent polls showed significant public opinion deeming both nominees unfit, but criticism has been particularly intense toward Kim, as specific allegations of lobbying for the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety have emerged alongside controversies over small shareholder damages. Questions arise about whether these issues disqualify him from leading a ministry responsible for justice, fairness, and human rights.
Nonetheless, the Democratic Party actively defends Kim, with Representative Kim Min-seok calling him “a personnel decision that looks better the more you examine it.” Analysts suggest that while Yong was merely a “unity nominee” who could be replaced at any time, Kim’s potential withdrawal could destabilize the administration itself. A Democratic Party insider stated, “The Minister of Justice symbolizes the completion of prosecutorial reform, so there’s a sense of crisis that further retreat would be catastrophic.” Another party member added, “Whether Kim resigns or is appointed, approval ratings will drop. So the mood is, ‘We might as well appoint him.
